01About

I'm an Associate Manager in Genomics & Health Data Mining at the Regeneron Genetics Center. I came to genetics because it lets us study a ton of the causal mechanisms of disease, fixed (germline) or otherwise acquired (somatic), directly in people, and I stay for the moments where such findings nominate exciting new medicines for patients.

Most of my work is translational genetics: starting with association testing across large sequencing and proteomics cohorts, then using methods like statistical fine-mapping and colocalization to identify the causal genes driving those signals. I also pair this with phenome-wide analyses and machine learning to weigh the evidence for a target, including where else it might help and where it might cause harm, and I work closely with our therapeutic-area and preclinical colleagues to test those hypotheses.

I trained as a physicist, and I still approach biology the same way (though it doesn't always apply): looking for ways to reduce a problem to a system of variables and equations that can be tested. During my PhD in Applied Physics at the University of South Florida with Dr. Ghanim Ullah, I combined biophysical modeling of synapses with single-cell and spatial multi-omics to map cell-type-specific mechanisms of synaptic dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease.

Along the way I built reference-mapping and interpretable deep-learning pipelines for target discovery at Cajal Neuroscience, and, as a PhD intern at RGC, worked on linking GWAS loci to drug-response mechanisms in large-scale single-cell perturbation data.

Outside work you'll find me playing soccer, running, reading or gaming.

02What I work on

Statistical genetics & target discovery

Common and rare-variant association, fine-mapping, colocalization and phenome-wide scans across exome and proteomics cohorts, used to find causal genes and judge which ones are worth a drug program.

Single-cell & network biology

snRNA-seq, scATAC-seq and spatial transcriptomics, co-expression and regulatory networks, and interpretable ML for cell states in neurodegeneration.

Mechanistic modeling

Biophysically detailed models of intracellular Ca²⁺ signaling and neurotransmitter release that turn molecular hypotheses into testable predictions.

Systematic Analysis of Biological Processes Reveals Gene Co-expression Modules Driving Pathway Dysregulation in Alzheimer's Disease

Adeoye T, Shah SI, Ullah G

Integrating pathway activity with single-nucleus co-expression networks across three brain regions shows that differential expression alone misses much of Alzheimer's biology, and implicates astrocytes and microglia in calcium, glutamate, lipid and TOR signaling.

Upregulated Ca²⁺ Release from the Endoplasmic Reticulum Leads to Impaired Presynaptic Function in Familial Alzheimer's Disease

Adeoye T, Shah SI, Demuro A, Rabson DA, Ullah G
