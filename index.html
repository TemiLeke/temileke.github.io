01About

I'm an Associate Manager in Genomics & Health Data Mining at the Regeneron Genetics Center, where I build analyses and models that turn human genetic and multi-omic evidence into prioritized therapeutic targets.

I trained as a physicist, and I still approach biology the same way: find the variables, write down the equations, and test what falls out. During my PhD in Applied Physics at the University of South Florida with Dr. Ghanim Ullah, I combined biophysical modeling of synapses with single-cell and spatial multi-omics to map cell-type-specific mechanisms of synaptic dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease.

Along the way I built reference-mapping and interpretable deep-learning pipelines for target discovery at Cajal Neuroscience, and, as a PhD intern at RGC, worked on linking GWAS loci to drug-response mechanisms in large-scale single-cell perturbation data.

Outside work you'll find me playing soccer, running, reading or gaming.

02What I work on

Genetics-driven target discovery

Integrating population-scale genetics, proteomics and phenome-wide data with machine learning to nominate and prioritize therapeutic targets, and to anticipate efficacy, new indications and safety.

Single-cell & network biology

snRNA-seq, scATAC-seq and spatial transcriptomics, co-expression and regulatory networks, and interpretable ML for cell states in neurodegeneration.

Mechanistic modeling

Biophysically detailed models of intracellular Ca²⁺ signaling and neurotransmitter release that turn molecular hypotheses into testable predictions.
